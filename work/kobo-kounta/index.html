Kobo Kounta

Helping Nigerians turn everyday money decisions into a consistent savings habit.

Role
Product Designer
Focus
Product strategy, UX & UI design
Status
Version 1.0 · Design in review
Direction
A daily loop: track, review, save, celebrate

Saving needs a daily structure

Kobo Kounta starts with a familiar gap: knowing that you should save is different from having a system that helps you do it. Informal saving traditions such as ajo, esusu and adashi already show how familiar the idea of saving is in Nigeria. The product opportunity is in what happens between one contribution and the next.

Without a regular view of spending, it is difficult to spot patterns or decide what to change. I shaped the product around a repeatable cycle: track spending, review the picture, put money towards a goal, and recognise progress.

The design question: how can checking your money become a small daily habit, rather than a task you avoid until something goes wrong?

Different incomes, the same need for clarity

The primary audience is working adults in Nigeria, aged 22–40, who have a smartphone and understand the value of saving but need more structure around it. Their circumstances change what that structure should look like.

A salaried worker needs to plan for recurring bills. A busy executive needs a quick view of a more complicated financial picture. A freelancer needs room for income that does not arrive on the same date every month.

Those differences informed the scope: expense tracking and a clear financial snapshot come first, with savings goals that connect everyday decisions to larger commitments.

Make the first step feel manageable

The early flows establish the promise before asking the user to build a new habit. The launch screen introduces the brand, and the welcome screen makes the purpose direct: track your money and work towards your goals.

I set a design target of reaching the Financial Snapshot within five minutes of downloading the app. The first session should make the product useful quickly, without turning setup into another chore.

A picture you can act on

The Financial Snapshot is the centre of the experience. It is where the information a user records needs to become something they can understand and use, rather than simply another list of transactions.

The design principles are practical: keep expense logging to three taps or fewer, bring relevant insights to the user, and explain calculations in plain language. I use these criteria to keep each flow focused on the next useful action.

Local currency, recurring obligations and Nigerian financial context are part of the product’s foundation. They should shape the experience from the start.

Build around the next useful action

The proposed habit loop connects four actions: track, review, save and celebrate. Logging makes spending visible. Reviewing helps someone notice a pattern. A savings goal gives that insight somewhere to go. Recognising progress gives them a reason to return.

The first-release scope prioritises expense tracking, financial analysis, savings goals, notifications and the dashboard, alongside authentication and secure storage. Investment prompts and debt tracking are secondary priorities; financial education is deferred.

Keeping those priorities explicit protects the core experience. The product needs to make the everyday loop useful before asking users to adopt more features.

Where the design stands

Kobo Kounta version 1.0 is in product design and review. The work shown here establishes the brand, onboarding direction and financial overview. There are no launch or adoption results to report yet.

One open design problem is keeping expense entry easy across platforms. The current scope explores SMS-assisted logging alongside a faster manual-entry route. An offline quick-add widget is also being scoped so a transaction can be recorded without making connectivity a prerequisite.

The next step is to refine those entry flows and examine whether the proposed daily loop is clear and useful in practice. The standard I am designing towards is simple: less effort to understand your money, and a clearer next action.
